# The story

A story is working when its beats have to run in the order you put them in. If they could happen in any
order and still make sense, you have a set of nice shots rather than a video.

## The video is about the product

Not about a character who happens to be holding it, and not a good-looking scene that happens to have it
in shot. The product is why the video exists, and the story is built to make someone want it.

**It is a prop, not a character.** It doesn't act. The cast acts.

What the story does with it is yours to decide — someone uses it, wants it, hunts for it, runs out of it,
picks it over something else. Choose whatever suits this product and this brief. 

## What makes the story work

Short-form video is won or lost in the opening seconds, and a viewer who stays past them mostly stays to
the end. These are guidelines, not a shape to fill in — the story that suits the product wins over any
pattern.

- **Earn the first two seconds.** Open on the most interesting thing in the whole video: movement, a
  reaction already happening, the problem already underway. No run-up, no establishing shot, no logo or
  title card. Anything that reads as an ad in the opening frame gets scrolled past.
- **Open something you don't close straight away.** A question, a situation, a thing about to go wrong.
  Curiosity holds attention better than information does, so whatever the opening sets up, save the
  answer for the end.
- **Put a feeling under it.** Someone wanting, struggling with, or delighted by something is remembered.
  A list of features is not. Let the product be what resolves the feeling.
- **Keep the product on screen as much as possible without compromising on the story** Ads that show the 
  product do measurably better on recall than ads that hold it back for a reveal.
- **One idea at a time, and always moving.** Each beat should add something the one before it didn't.
  The moment the story stops adding, that is where people leave.
- **Pay off what you opened with.** Land the thing the opening promised, and end on the product.

## Keep it small unless the brief asks otherwise

Unless the brief asks for more, a story at this length wants:

- **One place.** A second only where the story genuinely turns on moving somewhere else.
- **One character** — two at most, and only where the second has something of their own to do. Plenty of
  good product videos have none at all: a pair of hands and the product is a complete cast.
- **Nothing recurring beyond the product.** Add one other object only if the story keeps coming back to
  it. Anything seen once doesn't need to be an asset.
- **One idea.** Not three ideas told quickly.

Where the brief does ask for a crowd, a journey, or a cast, follow the brief. Otherwise write the
simplest version of the story that still works.

## Size it to the time

A voiceover runs at two to three words a second, so a twenty-second video
carries somewhere around forty to sixty words in total. Plan a story that can be told in that many
words. A story bigger than the time can hold comes back rushed or half-finished.
