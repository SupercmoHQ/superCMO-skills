# Google ad copy — Search / Responsive Search Ads

You don't write a finished ad — you supply parts and Google's system assembles them, shuffling
headlines and descriptions into combinations over time. So the craft is **independence**: every line
has to work on its own and next to any other.

## The fields you fill

- **Headlines** — minimum 3, up to **15**. Each is **≤30 characters** (a hard cap; the field rejects
  more).
- **Descriptions** — minimum 2, up to **4**. Each is **≤90 characters** (hard cap).
- **Display path** — up to two optional path fields, **≤15 characters** each.
- **No manual call-to-action button.** Google assembles the ad. (Performance Max has a separate CTA
  asset — automated, or picked from Google's list — not free text.)

Double-width characters (CJK) count as two. Supply **many** — Google recommends at least 8–10 distinct
headlines and as many descriptions as you can — so the system has variety to test. But only as many as
you can keep genuinely distinct: a thin brief yields fewer, and 9–10 headlines that avoid repeating the
same word beat 15 that all lean on it.

## How it assembles — and the independence rule

Typically up to 3 headlines and 2 descriptions show, in varying order and pairing. Because any headline
can land in any slot beside any other:

- **Each headline reads correctly alone and in any combination.** No headline that only makes sense as
  a continuation of another.
- **Make each one unique** — a different angle (a benefit, an offer, the brand, urgency, a
  keyword-match), not the same idea reworded. Repetition of the same word or phrase across assets is
  disallowed and wastes slots.
- **Pinning** forces a line to a fixed position, but it limits the combinations Google can test and
  lowers Ad Strength — pin only text that legally must appear in every ad, and pin as little as
  possible.

Aim for **Good or Excellent Ad Strength**; the lift from Poor to Excellent is real.

## The editorial rules — stricter than social

Google actively rejects hype formatting:

- **No emoji** — they are invalid characters in Google ad text.
- **No repeated punctuation** ("Sale!!!"), no symbols standing in for letters ("f1owers", "fl@wers"),
  no gimmicky spacing ("F.L.O.W.E.R.S"), no non-standard bullets.
- **No excessive or mixed caps** ("FLOWERS", "FlOwErS") — exceptions are common abbreviations (ASAP),
  coupon codes, and brand/product names with established styling.
- Exclamation marks are heavily restricted (generally none in headlines).

## Register and craft

Match the searcher's intent and be literal: front-load the keyword or benefit, be specific and factual
(prices, offers, differentiators). The register is restrained and clean — Google's policies penalise
the hype punctuation that works elsewhere. Give the machine range: cover features, offers, brand,
urgency and keyword-match across your headline set.

## Before handing back

- Every headline ≤30, every description ≤90 — counted, not estimated; over is a rewrite.
- Each headline stands alone and reads in any combination; all are distinct angles.
- No emoji, no repeated punctuation, no gimmick caps.
- Minimal or no pinning.
