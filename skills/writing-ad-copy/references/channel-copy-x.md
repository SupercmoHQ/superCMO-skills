# X (Twitter) ad copy — Promoted ad

Brevity is the medium. One sharp idea in a voice that reads like a person posting, not a banner.

## The fields you fill

- **Post text** — the body above the image. Carries the hook.
- **Media headline** — the bar on the image card when the ad drives to a site or app.
- **Description** — a secondary line under the headline on some card formats; not always present.

The call to action comes from the card/objective, not a free-text button.

## Limits and the fold

| Field | Budget | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Post text | **280 characters** hard | a link consumes ~23, leaving ~257 for words |
| Media headline | **≤50 characters** recommended (70 hard) | ~2 lines on the card; mobile cuts ~50–55 |
| Description | ~125 recommended (≈200 max) | secondary; verify in Ads Manager |

Post text rarely truncates at 280, but the **first line is what earns the tap** — front-load the hook.
Keep the headline ≤50 so it survives the card crop on mobile. The description number is a secondary
figure — confirm live.

## Calls to action

Attached through the card/objective — Install, Open, Play, Shop, Book, Connect, Order and similar.
**Conversation Buttons** are also available (custom buttons that open a pre-filled reply post). The
exact set is configured in the Ads Manager and changes; confirm live.

## Register and craft

Conversational, punchy, culturally current, slightly informal — a person's voice, not ad-speak.
Lowercase, wit, and timeliness read as native. Open with the hook in the first few words; a question or
a bold claim beats a feature list. Hashtags and @handles cost characters and rarely help a paid post,
so use them sparingly. Let the card and its CTA carry the "click here"; the post text carries the
tension.

## Before handing back

- Post text ≤280 (allow ~23 for a link); hook in the first line; headline ≤50.
- Reads like a native post, one clear idea, not a banner.
- Minimal hashtags/@handles.
