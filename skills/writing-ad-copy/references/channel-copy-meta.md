# Meta ad copy — Facebook & Instagram

A finished, human-written ad: you control the exact words and pick the button. Write for the feed's
truncation, and stay clear of the personal-attributes policy.

## The fields you fill

- **Primary text** — the body above the image. Carries the hook, the proof and the offer.
- **Headline** — the bold line by the button. One concrete promise.
- **Description** — a link description shown in some placements only; often not rendered on mobile
  feed, so never put anything load-bearing here.
- **Call to action** — a button chosen from Meta's preset set, not free text.

**Fill both primary text and headline every time.** Placement decides what shows: Facebook feed shows
the primary text and the headline; Instagram feed shows the primary text only; Instagram Stories shows
the headline only. A field left empty drops out of that placement.

## Limits and the fold

Meta publishes these as **recommendations tied to truncation**, not hard entry caps — you can type more,
but the feed hides it. Treat the recommended number as the working budget, and **verify live in Ads
Manager, since Meta doesn't publish exact hard numbers and they drift.**

| Field | Working budget | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary text | ~125 characters before the fold | collapses behind "… See more"; most people never expand |
| Headline | ~27 characters | truncates on feed past this |
| Description | ~30 characters | frequently not shown on mobile — optional, never load-bearing |

**Front-load everything.** The hook, the brand and the offer sit in the first ~125 characters of the
primary text, before "… See more". The headline's core promise fits inside its ~27.

## Calls to action

A preset set, filtered by the campaign objective — common ones include Shop Now, Learn More, Sign Up,
Subscribe, Download, Book Now, Get Offer, Order Now, Get Quote, Contact Us. Pick the one that names the
real next step; there is no custom button text. Confirm the current roster in Ads Manager.

## The personal-attributes policy — the copywriter's landmine

Ad copy must not assert or imply you know a person's **protected attribute** — race, ethnicity,
religion, age, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health, financial
status, or name. The trap is second-person copy that implies "we know *this* about you."

- **Rejected:** "Are you a Christian?", "Meet other diabetics", "Struggling with debt?", "Billy, get
  your name on this shirt."
- **Fine:** "you" / "your" not tied to a protected attribute, and broad geography ("New Yorkers love
  this").

Rework a claim about the buyer into a claim about the product or the outcome.

## Register and craft

Conversational, first- or second-person, one clear idea. Lead with the hook and the offer in the first
line. Benefit-forward and native to the feed rather than salesy. Emoji are allowed (Meta sets no hard
cap) — use them to punctuate, not to decorate. The old 20%-text-on-image rule is gone, but a
text-heavy image can still suppress delivery, so keep the *selling* in these copy fields, not baked
into the picture.

## Before handing back

- Primary text hook + offer land before the ~125-char fold; headline promise inside ~27.
- Both primary text and headline filled.
- No copy implies a protected attribute about the reader.
- The CTA names the real next step.
